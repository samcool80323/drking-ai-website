DrKing combines AI-assisted conversations, shared lead management and follow-up campaigns. Your team stays responsible for clinical judgement, permission to contact and the final appointment record.

01 / CONNECT

Keep the conversation.

Bring enquiries from connected channels into a shared view. Website chat can start a conversation; phone, email and SMS can continue it when configured. Plan supported social campaigns alongside them, with a named owner for replies.

02 / RECONNECT

Follow up with permission.

Plan SMS and email campaigns for existing contacts, including periodic reactivation for patients who have agreed to be contacted. Segment carefully, handle opt-outs and have a human review the message and timing. Social campaign planning can sit alongside that follow-up.

When your team is with a patient

Different calls need different owners.

A shared conversation is useful only when someone knows what to do next. DrKing can support communication; the practice defines the callback, booking and clinical escalation rules.

01 / MISSED CALL

Who calls back?

Name the person or team, the callback window and what happens when the caller cannot be reached. Check the practice’s contact and consent rules before using any message channel.

02 / BOOKING REQUEST

Who checks the diary?

Record the requested appointment type and preferred time without promising a slot. Staff should confirm it against the practice’s system of record, provider and duration rules.

03 / CLINICAL CONCERN

Who takes the handoff?

Agree a human escalation path for pain, bleeding, complications or other urgent concerns. Do not let a generic call script make clinical decisions.

Check your practice software

A request is not a booking.

Before choosing a call-handling tool, decide which system is authoritative and how a human checks exceptions.

  • Source of truth: Which diary holds the appointment, and can staff see requests that did not write back?
  • Scheduling rules: Who validates clinician, chair, appointment type, duration and availability?
  • Failure path: What happens if a slot changes, a sync fails or the caller changes their mind?
  • Records boundary: Which details may be taken on a call, who may access them, and what stays in the clinical record?
  • Contact permission: Who approves callbacks or messages, checks consent and handles opt-outs?

An advertised integration does not establish what your software version, configuration or contract supports. Ask for a test against your actual practice workflow.

Can a booking request be treated as confirmed?

No. Confirmation should follow a successful check in the practice’s authoritative diary and the practice’s rules for provider, chair and duration. Ask a supplier to show what happens when that check fails.

What should happen when a caller raises a clinical concern?

Set a human escalation route and a fallback for when that person is unavailable. The practice must define its own clinical and urgent-care policy; this guide does not provide clinical advice.

What should we test before changing the call journey?

Test an unanswered call, booking request, reschedule, failed software sync and urgent handoff. Review call routing, permission to contact, recording and retention with the appropriate practice owners.

Will DrKing automatically confirm appointments in our practice software?

Do not assume that taking a booking request confirms an appointment. Supported integration, configuration and confirmation steps vary by practice. Ask to test your software and its handoff before relying on automatic booking.
