# DrKing brand-awareness journal direction — proposal, not a release

**Owner correction (2026-10-30):** The live pilot-plan and missed-call-audit pages were rejected as irrelevant/not worthwhile. Treat this as a hard content-acceptance failure, not a prompt to clone them in blog format. They remain live until the owner decides their disposition; do not republish, promote or claim they are accepted. This brief is local only.

## What the posts should do
Speak to Australian clinic owners and practice managers in DrKing's voice: thoughtful, human, practical, not a software feature list or downloadable worksheet. Help readers see the work and feeling behind a patient's first contact; connect to DrKing only after earning the reader's attention. No invented patients, interviews, product demos, outcomes, clinical advice, conversion percentages or guarantees. A fictional scene, if used, must be explicitly labelled illustrative. Verify any concrete DrKing workflow against the product owner before publishing.

## Recommended first post
**Working title:** “The hidden work behind a patient's first hello”

**Angle:** A magazine-style look at what a reception team balances when someone reaches out: the question, the context the caller needs, the staff member's interrupted work, and the next clear step. This celebrates clinic staff rather than suggesting AI replaces them. It is a brand-awareness story, not another missed-call calculator or test plan.

**Shape:**
1. Opening: a clearly illustrative, unnamed moment when a clinic has a person at the desk and another trying to reach the team. No claim that this happened at a DrKing customer.
2. Why the first exchange matters as a design choice: plain language, clarity about what happens next, and an easy route to a person when needed. These are recommendations, not measured outcomes.
3. The less visible work: asking, recording, routing and following through; distinguish administrative communication from clinical judgment.
4. DrKing viewpoint: technology should support a clear next step and leave staff responsible for exceptions. Name specific product features only after first-party verification.
5. Soft conclusion and relevant link to `/about` or `/demo`; not a hard-sell block in every section.

**Original graphic:** A wide, bespoke illustrated “conversation journey” built as accessible SVG: caller question → answer or acknowledgement → human handoff where needed → clear next step. A second small editorial pull-quote/card can show the two perspectives (caller and reception team). Labels describe a general clinic process, not a guaranteed DrKing workflow. No fabricated screenshots or patient photos.

## Second post, only if the first direction lands
**Working title:** “Where the human touch belongs in AI-assisted clinic conversations”

**Angle:** A calm, honest point of view on division of labour: routine administrative information versus a real person for sensitive, uncertain or clinical concerns. Not a buyer checklist, competitor comparison, or claim that DrKing can perform a particular escalation. It answers the trust objection at a brand level and differs from the first post's reception-team narrative.

**Original graphic:** Two-column editorial illustration, “supported by technology” and “owned by a person,” with a connective handoff line. Include alt text and a text explanation for readers who cannot see it. Any example must be clearly hypothetical until a tested DrKing workflow is supplied.

Prefer original SVG editorial illustrations in this palette over generic AI robot imagery, stock photos presented as real staff, emoji icons, fake dashboards or crowded cards. Make desktop/mobile article layout and social-share crop coherent; optimise SVGs and give meaningful alt text.
