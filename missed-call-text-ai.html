Clinic stories / 01

The call you missed. The conversation you didn’t.

Your front desk is already doing three things at once. The phone rings out. Here’s what can happen next — and why a short text, sent while the caller is still holding their phone, changes everything.

01 · The moment

It starts with a ring that goes unanswered.

Nobody plans to miss a call. Your receptionist is checking in a patient, or walking someone through a form, or simply taking a breath between tasks. The phone rings twice, three times, and then it stops.

For the caller, that silence is a small, quiet decision point. Do they call back later? Do they try the next practice on the list? Or do they just… move on? Most won’t leave a voicemail. The moment passes and your clinic never knows it happened.

That’s the problem worth solving — not answering every call with more staff, or working your team harder, but catching the moment before it disappears.

02 · The text

A short text, sent while they’re still holding the phone.

DrKing detects the missed call and sends a warm SMS to the caller within sixty seconds — before they’ve had time to ring anyone else. It’s a simple message that acknowledges the call and offers the next step: “Sorry we missed your call. Would you like to book an appointment?”

It feels like a person, not a system. Because it should. The caller is still thinking about your clinic — the text meets them exactly there.

The best time to answer a missed call is while the caller is still looking at their phone.

03 · The conversation

From a quick reply to a confirmed booking.

When the patient replies, the conversation continues naturally. They ask about times. DrKing checks your practice’s availability and offers real slots. They pick one. The appointment is confirmed and the details flow into your calendar and CRM — the same records your team already uses.

No phone tag. No “we’ll call you back.” Just a short exchange that ends with a patient who knows when they’re coming in and a front desk that didn’t have to chase anything.

And when the enquiry is something the text conversation can’t resolve — a clinical question, a concern that needs a person — it’s flagged for your team with the context, so the follow-up starts informed rather than from zero.

05 · The point

A missed call isn’t the end of the conversation.

It used to be. The phone would ring, nobody would answer, and both sides would move on — one to the next practice, the other to the next task, neither aware of what almost happened.

A short text changes the shape of that moment. The caller feels acknowledged. The practice stays in the conversation. And the appointment that might have quietly disappeared gets booked while it still matters.

That’s the whole idea: not more software in your day, but a small bridge over the gap that already exists between “we missed you” and “see you tomorrow.”
